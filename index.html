Selected work

Three robots, two competitions, one constraint: in every game, the robot that moves less mass wins more cycles. Each one is built from pocketed aluminum plate.

FTC · Into the Deep · 2024–25

CrimsonA small, light robot that reaches into the fenced submersible and pulls samples out fast.

Team
Bolt.m3 #22801
My role
Lead engineer & Connect manager
Result
First KZ team at a Premier Event

Problem

Game pieces sat inside a fenced submersible zone. A robot that can reach in from any side, grab, and transfer without repositioning wins cycles.

What I did

  • Led the design through three revisions, from Crimson 1.0 to 3.0, against a 2 m/s speed target, cutting width and weight each round.
  • Packaged a horizontal extension (1150 rpm motor) with a motor-driven active intake low enough to reach the submersible from any side.
  • CNC-machined, pocketed aluminum parallel-plate drivetrain on mecanum wheels. Its odometry pods retract on a cable-pulley, the way a tendon moves a prosthetic finger.
  • Led the team's Connect work. At the Premier Event, we won the Connect Award, 1st place.

FTC · DECODE · 2025–26

FoxslideA turret-shooter robot that moves three balls from intake to launcher in 0.17 s.

Team
Foxslide #29029
My role
CAD & engineering handbook
Result
Playoffs, 2026 FIRST Championship

Problem

DECODE is scored by collecting balls and launching them into a goal. Transfer time between intake and shooter is paid on every cycle, so it set the ceiling on our score.

What I did

  • Worked on the robot's CAD through five versions in one season, from kickoff to the Worlds robot.
  • Wrote the engineering handbook that documents the design.

Design highlights (team)

  • Compliant intake wheels cut transfer time from 0.8 s to 0.17 s.
  • Belts replaced chains and bevel gears on every stage, raising overall efficiency by 9%.
  • A power-take-off (PTO) and bridging mechanism for end-game parking, which pushed our skills in complex CAD joint modeling.
  • Custom 500 rpm parallel-plate drivetrain for defense, cut from domestic B95 aluminum with Voronoi pocketing.

Crimson drivetrain: three revisions

FTC Into the Deep · 2024–25 · lead engineer
Criterion: ≥ 2 m/s Criterion: reach the submersible from any side Criterion: move around barriers smoothly
Rev 1.0Failed
Problem
Too wide. The structure was inefficient.
Change
Replaced the 10-hole U-channels joining the two halves with 7-hole U-channels.
Rev 2.0Failed
Problem
Too heavy. Too many U-channels, and a rear-heavy center of mass made the robot tip.
Change
Moved drive motors forward to balance the lifts and arm. Replaced three low-profile U-channels with one full U-channel and aluminum sidewalls.
Rev 3.0Criteria met
Result
CNC-machined parallel-plate aluminum chassis, mecanum wheels, retractable odometry pods, and a balanced center of mass.
Lesson
Where the mass sits matters as much as motor power, for both speed and stability.
